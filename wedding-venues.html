Turnkey, semi, DIY — what each word means here

Fred is happy with DIY. Fred is not happy with "it's Thursday and we're making 94 centrepieces." These definitions keep the two apart. House rule: DIY deadline is 30 days before the wedding — anything unfinished gets bought, delegated, simplified or dropped.

Full turnkey

One contract covers space, food, bar, tables, chairs, linens, setup, teardown, staff and lodging. You choose menus and bring your own vendors for photo, music, officiant and childcare.

  • Le Germain · Cap-aux-Pierres · Manoir Richelieu · Cap au Leste · Château Montebello
  • Your DIY: paperless invites, signage, playlist, light florals, cameras, cake

Semi-turnkey

Venue handles food and bar in its own restaurant, but ceremony setup, dance space, overflow lodging or a rain plan need outside pieces you organize.

  • None on the current list (this was the Poste de Traite / La Goéliche model)
  • Your DIY would be: everything above + ceremony chairs, sound, lodging guide, shuttle

DIY-heavy

Venue provides the site and maybe its own product (wine). You contract a caterer, rentals and bar service separately. Beautiful, personal and a second job.

  • Isle de Bacchus (probable)
  • Your DIY: caterer, tent, tables, chairs, linens, lighting, power, washrooms, bar staff, shuttle

Full DIY

A raw location — field, family land, a rented house. Nothing exists until you bring it. Not on the list, and given the "no last-minute projects" rule, it should stay off.

  • None of the current venues
  • Only viable with a professional planner running it end to end
